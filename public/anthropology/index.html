Paper I · 250 Marks

Theoretical & Physical Anthropology

Evolutionary biology, primatology, prehistoric archaeology and social theory — the four specimen groups that make up the first paper, taught as one continuous record rather than four separate subjects.

Unit — Primatology

Primates & the Living Record

  • Primate characteristics & classification
  • Comparative anatomy: Pongidae & Hominidae
  • Primate behaviour & social organisation
  • Living major primates & their conservation status
Unit — Physical Anthropology

Human Evolution & Variation

  • Biological & cultural evolution theories
  • Human genetics: Mendelian & population genetics
  • Race & racism, human growth & nutrition
  • Fossil evidence: Australopithecines to Homo sapiens
Unit — Archaeological Anthropology

Prehistory & Material Culture

  • Paleolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic traditions
  • Dating methods: relative & absolute chronology
  • Tool typology & technology through the ages
  • Origin of agriculture & domestication
Unit — Sociocultural Theory

Anthropological Thought

  • Evolutionism, diffusionism, functionalism
  • Structuralism & post-structuralism
  • Kinship, marriage & family systems
  • Economic & political organisation

Paper I rewards sequence. We teach it in the order fossils were laid down.

Paper II · 250 Marks

Indian Anthropology

Read as a set of field notebook entries — from the peopling of India to the tribal situation today, each entry is written the way you'll need to answer it: with region, community and current relevance attached.

Entry 01 — Prehistoric & Ethnic Foundations

The Peopling of India

Paleolithic to Neolithic cultures on the subcontinent, and the racial and linguistic classifications historically used to describe Indian populations.

Caste, Tribe & Village India

Mains Test Series

Excavated in four phases.

Every test is evaluated with the same rubric UPSC examiners use: content accuracy, structure, diagrams and time discipline — so your score on test one is comparable to your score on test forty.

01

Foundation Layer

Topic-wise tests on each unit of Paper I and Paper II, taken right after that unit is taught. Builds recall before it builds speed.

16 SECTIONAL TESTS · 20 MARKS EACH
02

Sedimentary Layer

Full-syllabus questions mixed across units, forcing you to connect theory with case material — exactly how UPSC frames real questions.

10 MIXED TESTS · 100 MARKS EACH
03

Compression Layer

Full-length, three-hour simulated Mains papers under exam conditions, with the same time pressure and question count as the real exam.

10 FULL-LENGTH TESTS · 250 MARKS EACH
04

Surface Layer

Final revision tests built entirely from your own past mistakes and weak topics, taken in the last six weeks before the exam.
